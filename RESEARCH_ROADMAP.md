# World Mechanics — Open Research Roadmap

## Paper I — Published package ready
**AI as an Instrument for Describing Reality: Representation, Prediction, and the Limits of Machine Understanding**

Foundation paper defining the five-level World Mechanics framework: representation, prediction, intervention, mechanistic explanation and subjective experience.

## Paper II — Language as an Interface to Machine Intelligence
Research question: when humans control models through natural language, what is language doing as a computational interface, and how should apparent reasoning be measured independently of self-reported explanations?

Planned contribution: operational definitions, reproducible prompting/evaluation protocol, and a small open-model experiment comparing answer correctness with explanation faithfulness.

## Paper III — AI for a Reproducible Quantum Toy System
Research question: how far can a classical learned representation predict observables or phases in a small simulated quantum system, and where does the learned representation fail?

Planned contribution: simulator, baseline models, held-out tests, calibration/error analysis, containerised execution and public provenance.

## Paper IV — Biological Representations as Scientific Instruments
Research question: when biological foundation-model embeddings predict a useful target, what evidence supports representation, correlation and mechanism as distinct claims?

Planned contribution: open dataset, simple baseline, open foundation-model embeddings, controlled evaluation and explicit limits on causal interpretation.

## Paper V — From Scientific Copilot to Observable Research System
Research question: how should AI-assisted scientific workflows preserve evidence, provenance, reproducibility and human accountability?

Planned contribution: an engineering reference architecture for open AI-assisted research, including data lineage, source binding, experiment versioning, evaluation artefacts and publication metadata.

## Release discipline
Each release should include source code, exact environment/dependencies, dataset licence and provenance, random seeds where applicable, evaluation outputs, negative results, limitations, citation metadata, and an explicit AI-assistance statement.
