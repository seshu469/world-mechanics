Three claims

01 · Physics firstQuantum mechanics is still the public language of the microscopic. AI is a search and representation layer on top of it, not a new metaphysics.
02 · Narrow quantum biologyThe mature cases are enzymatic tunnelling and radical-pair magnetoreception. Warm-tissue “coherence as magic” is not earned.
03 · AsymmetryHuman intelligence is not derived from AI. AI can be understood. Present systems have no evidenced emotions.

What talking to a model actually is

The interface looks like conversation. The mechanism is next-token prediction plus whatever tools you bolt on. Fluency is cheap to mistake for a person.

SaidMore accurate
It understandsIt matches and generalises patterns of competent use.
It thinksIt runs multi-step computation in weights, activations, and generated tokens.
It feelsNo evidenced body, interoception, or animal-like valence system.
Humans came from AIFalse. Brains evolved. Models were trained on human traces.

AI as quantum instrument

Many-body quantum states explode with particle number. The 2025–2026 literature uses networks as surrogates: property prediction, state reconstruction, neural wave functions, and generative models that propose molecular circuits (including work involving Quantinuum, NVIDIA, and Pfizer on drug-like molecules).

The disciplined posture is: treat the model as a fallible junior theorist. Force outputs through symmetries, conservation, and numerical checks. That is the lesson of “AI physicist” benchmarks and multi-stage code workflows.

Life sciences without the mysticism

Protein models and generative design are now routine inputs. Cellular-scale generative AI is still a list of hard unsolved problems. The honest join to quantum is mostly quantum for biology (simulate the molecule) and a short list of quantum in biology effects that survive warm, wet conditions.

A working map

  1. World — one physical world.
  2. Description — Hamiltonians, models, diagrams that earn their keep against measurement.
  3. Instrument — sequencers, quantum processors, neural nets.
  4. Interface language — words, code, circuits. Easy to confuse with a mind.
  5. Understanding — checkable prediction and localisable failure. Humans also feel. Machines, so far, do not.
